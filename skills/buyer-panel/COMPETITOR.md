# Competitor

Add a competitor or change one's price, re-run the same buyers in fresh contexts, and show who switched, who stayed and the reasons that held them. `P` is the panel folder, `.buyer-panel/<slug>/`; `panel.py` is `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panel.py"`.

Read `RECORDS.md` before step 1, `INTERVIEW.md` before step 3, `SCORING.md` before step 5 and `READING.md` before step 7 (all in this folder).

1. **Base round.** Pick the panel (one in the ledger: that one; several: ask with options, the most recent first). The base round is the last round in `P/history.md` shown to every buyer. Run `panel.py aggregate P/rounds/<base> --out P/rounds/<base>/summary.json` if the summary is missing. The new round is `r<k+1>` after the highest `r<k>`. No panel yet: say `competitor` needs a first round and offer `run`. Done when the base summary exists.
2. **Competitor.** The text after `competitor` names it and, for a reprice, the new price. Missing: ask with options: **add a competitor** (name the ones from `research.md` the base round did not show, the most used first), **reprice one** (name the base round's alternatives with their prices), **stop**. An added competitor gets a real price, plan and source in `research.md` (RUN.md step 3, the browser question first when a pricing page must be opened); a price the user sets as a what-if goes into `research.md` marked hypothetical, given by the user. The competitor's id is its name, never `offer` or `nothing`. Show its card (INTERVIEW.md, the cards) at the new price and ask with options: **run it** (recommended), **change the price**, **stop**. Done when the user chose to run it.
3. **Round.** Create `P/rounds/r<k+1>/` with a `round.json` that copies the base's (the offer and its price unchanged), adds the competitor's id to `options` when it is added, and adds `base` (the base label) and `competitor`: `{"name", "price"}`, plus `"was"` (its price in the base round) for a reprice. The cards show every alternative at the price this round gives it. Deal with `panel.py deal --buyers P/buyers.jsonl --options <ids> --batch-size <n> --out P/rounds/r<k+1>/cards.jsonl` and copy the seed. Done when `round.json` validates (the script checks the competitor is one of the options and not a reserved id) and `cards.jsonl` holds one line per buyer.
4. **Interviews.** RUN.md step 9 on the whole panel, every buyer in a fresh context that saw no earlier round: with orchestrate, job `buyer-panel-<slug>-r<k+1>`, profile `light`, state dir `P/rounds/r<k+1>/`, units = the batches in `cards.jsonl`, unit brief = INTERVIEW.md's worker brief with this round's cards, each worker writing only `P/rounds/r<k+1>/answers/batch-<n>.jsonl`; without it, one buyer at a time with `interviews: inline`. Done when every buyer has a valid answer.
5. **Judge and numbers.** RUN.md steps 10 and 11 on the round: its own judge context, `scores.jsonl`, `aggregate --out summary.json`. Done when `aggregate` exits 0.
6. **Diff.** `panel.py competitor P/rounds/<base> P/rounds/r<k+1> --out P/rounds/r<k+1>/diff.json`, then once more without `--json` for the Markdown. It prints the round diff, then the competitor: added or repriced, how many chose it before and after, who switched to it and from what, who left it and for what, and who stayed on each option with the reasons that held them, each with the quote from this round. Exit 2 names what `round.json` is missing (no `competitor`, or a reprice without `was`). Done when it exits 0.
7. **Report.** Write `P/rounds/r<k+1>/report.md` per READING.md, with four sections between the summary and the verdict: **The competitor** (what changed, at what price, and the `research.md` line or the user's what-if behind it); **Who switched** (the buyers who moved to it, or left it after a reprice, each with the option they came from or went to, the reason and the quote, as the script printed them); **Who stayed** (per option, the buyers and the reasons that held them); **What changed** (the diff's Markdown, unchanged). A net change of one or two buyers is noise at this size: write that, never "the competitor takes the market". The verdict says whether the offer holds against the competitor and what holds the buyers who stayed with it; the first real test probes that reason on real users of the competitor (what they pay, what would make them switch).
8. **Sweep.** With ai-slop-prose installed, run its sweep on `report.md` with purpose `panel report`; it may rewrite the prose, never a number, quote or table row. Without it, the method section says the report was not swept.
9. **Brief and history.** A real competitor at its real price joins `P/brief.json`'s alternatives (or takes its new price there), so later rounds show it; a what-if stays in this round only. Append one line to `P/history.md`: date, `r<k+1>`, `competitor <name>` and the base label, the competitor's price before and after, raw and discounted BUY share, PASS to BUY and BUY to PASS, the buyers who switched to it, the verdict.

**Completion.** The round validates and aggregates with exit 0; every buyer was interviewed in a fresh context; `diff.json` from `panel.py competitor` and the report exist; the report opens with the simulated-panel line, shows who switched, who stayed and the reasons that held them as the script printed them, and ends with a verdict and one real test; `research.md` has a source or a what-if mark for the competitor's price; the history has the round's line.
