# Competitor

Add a competitor, change one's price or take away one that stopped trading, re-run the same buyers in fresh contexts, and show who switched, who stayed and the reasons that held them. `P` is the panel folder, `.buyer-panel/<slug>/`; `panel.py` is `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panel.py"`.

Read `RECORDS.md` before step 1, `INTERVIEW.md` before step 3, `SCORING.md` before step 5 and `READING.md` and `CRITIQUE.md` before step 7 (all in this folder). Bold steps without a number (**Base round**, **Critique**, **Sweep**) are in SKILL.md, steps the modes share.

1. **Base round.** The **Base round**; the new round is `r<k+1>`. Done when the base summary exists.
2. **Competitor.** The text after `competitor` names it and, for a reprice, the new price. Missing: ask with options: **add a competitor** (name the ones from `research.md` the base round did not show, the most used first), **reprice one** (name the base round's alternatives with their prices), **remove one** (an alternative of the base round that stopped trading: RUN.md step 3 found no page still taking customers), **stop**. A removal needs the `research.md` line, with its source and date, that shows the alternative closed; without one, it stays on the cards. An added competitor gets a real price, plan and source in `research.md` (RUN.md step 3, the browser question first when a pricing page must be opened); a price the user sets as a what-if goes into `research.md` marked hypothetical, given by the user. The competitor's id is its name, never `offer` or `nothing`. Show its card (INTERVIEW.md, the cards) at the new price, or for a removal the base round's options without it, and ask with options: **run it** (recommended), **change the price** (not for a removal), **stop**. Done when the user chose to run it.
3. **Round.** Create `P/rounds/r<k+1>/` with a `round.json` that copies the base's (the offer and its price unchanged), adds the competitor's id to `options` when it is added, and adds `base` (the base label) and `competitor`: `{"name", "price"}`, plus `"was"` (its price in the base round) for a reprice. A removal drops the alternative from `options`, adds `base` and `change` (`removed <name>: stopped trading`) and no `competitor`; buyers who name it as their current solution keep it in `buyers.jsonl` (buyers are never edited), so their `nothing` card adds that it has stopped trading. The cards show every alternative at the price this round gives it. Deal with `panel.py deal --buyers P/buyers.jsonl --options <ids> --batch-size <n> --out P/rounds/r<k+1>/cards.jsonl` and copy the seed. Done when `round.json` validates (the script checks the competitor is one of the options and not a reserved id) and `cards.jsonl` holds one line per buyer.
4. **Interviews.** RUN.md step 9 on the whole panel, every buyer in a fresh context that saw no earlier round: with orchestrate, job `buyer-panel-<slug>-r<k+1>`, profile `light`, state dir `P/rounds/r<k+1>/`, units = the batches in `cards.jsonl`, unit brief = INTERVIEW.md's worker brief with this round's cards, each worker writing only `P/rounds/r<k+1>/answers/batch-<n>.jsonl`; without it, one buyer at a time with `interviews: inline`. Done when every buyer has a valid answer.
5. **Judge and numbers.** RUN.md steps 10 and 11 on the round: its own judge context, `scores.jsonl`, `aggregate --out summary.json`, its checks. Done when `aggregate` exits 0 and `checks.json` exists.
6. **Diff.** `panel.py competitor P/rounds/<base> P/rounds/r<k+1> --out P/rounds/r<k+1>/diff.json`, then once more without `--json` for the Markdown. It prints the round diff, then the competitor: added or repriced, how many chose it before and after, who switched to it and from what, who left it and for what, and who stayed on each option with the reasons that held them, each with the quote from this round. Exit 2 names what `round.json` is missing (no `competitor`, or a reprice without `was`). For a removal run `panel.py diff` with the same arguments instead (`competitor` needs the competitor among the options): its switches from the removed name show where its buyers went. Done when it exits 0.
7. **Report.** The **Critique** on `r<k+1>`, then write `P/rounds/r<k+1>/report.md` per READING.md, with its Checks section and four sections between the summary and the verdict: **The competitor** (what changed, at what price, and the `research.md` line or the user's what-if behind it); **Who switched** (the buyers who moved to it, or left it after a reprice, each with the option they came from or went to, the reason and the quote, as the script printed them); **Who stayed** (per option, the buyers and the reasons that held them); for a removal, **Where its buyers went** (the diff's switches from it, each with the reason and quote from its rows) in place of the two before; **What changed** (the diff's Markdown, unchanged). A net change of one or two buyers is noise at this size: write that, never "the competitor takes the market". The verdict says whether the offer holds against the competitor and what holds the buyers who stayed with it; the first real test probes that reason on real users of the competitor (what they pay, what would make them switch).
8. **Sweep.** The **Sweep** on `report.md`.
9. **Brief and history.** A real competitor at its real price joins `P/brief.json`'s alternatives (or takes its new price there), so later rounds show it; a removed one leaves them; a what-if stays in this round only. Append one line to `P/history.md`: date, `r<k+1>`, `competitor <name>` and the base label, the competitor's price before and after, raw and discounted BUY share, PASS to BUY and BUY to PASS, the buyers who switched to it, the verdict.

**Completion.** The round validates and aggregates with exit 0; every buyer was interviewed in a fresh context; `checks.json`, `critique.md`, `diff.json` from `panel.py competitor` (from `panel.py diff` for a removal) and the report exist; the report opens with the simulated-panel line, shows the Checks section and the devil's advocate, says "Do not trust this round" in its verdict when a check failed, shows who switched, who stayed and the reasons that held them as the script printed them, and ends with a verdict and one real test; `research.md` has a source or a what-if mark for the competitor's price, or for a removal the source showing it stopped trading; the history has the round's line.
