# Pitch

Write a 30-second pitch for the buyer type most likely to buy, or the one named in `$ARGUMENTS`, from that type's own words, then try it on buyers of that type it was not written from. `P` is the panel folder; `panel.py` is `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panel.py"`.

Read `INTERVIEW.md` before step 6, `SCORING.md` before step 7 and `READING.md` before step 8 (all in this folder).

1. **Source round.** The pitch comes from the last round shown to the whole panel: `r1`, or a later full round named in `P/history.md`. No scored round yet: say so and offer `run`. Pick the next free label `pitch-<n>` and create `P/rounds/pitch-<n>/`. Done when the source round's `aggregate` exits 0.
2. **Quotes.** `panel.py quotes P/rounds/<source> [--type <type>] --out P/rounds/pitch-<n>/quotes.json --holdout-out P/rounds/pitch-<n>/buyers.jsonl`; it prints the type, its words and who is held out, and writes both files. The script picks the type (the one named, or the first by discounted BUY share), holds out half of that type's buyers, dealt so both halves hold buyers who bought and buyers who passed, and lists only the other half's words, each with its buyer id. An unknown type exits 2 and names the types there are. Stop and ask with options when:
   - the type has no BUY: **pitch it anyway** (its pass reasons say what the pitch must answer), **pick another type** (name the next ones from `types`), **stop**;
   - nobody is held out (a type of one buyer): **write it untried** (the report says it was never tried), **pick another type**.

   Done when `quotes.json` exists and the type is settled.
3. **Pitch.** Read `quotes.json`'s `quotes` and nothing else about these buyers: never the held-out buyers' answers, never the source round's report. Write about 75 words (65 to 85), spoken aloud in 30 seconds, to that type: who it is for, the problem in their words, what the offer does, its price as the seller set it, one thing to do next. Every phrase borrowed from a buyer goes in double quotes, copied from that buyer's `quote` or `reaction`; take nothing from outside the list, and invent no praise. Write `P/rounds/pitch-<n>/pitch.md`: the pitch, its word count, and a trace table `| phrase | buyer | name |` with one row per quoted phrase. Done when the count is 65 to 85 and every quoted phrase appears (case and spacing aside) in the `quote` or `reaction` of the buyer its row names.
4. **Sweep.** With ai-slop-prose installed, run its sweep on `pitch.md` with purpose `pitch` (its PURPOSES.md row); it may rewrite the pitch's own words, never a quoted phrase or the trace. Recheck step 3's done criterion afterwards. Without it, `pitch.md` says it was not swept.
5. **Try-out round.** Write `P/rounds/pitch-<n>/round.json` from the source round's: same price, currency, options and model, label `pitch-<n>`, `offer` = the pitch with its quotation marks removed and no trace (buyers see a seller's pitch, not who said what). Then `panel.py deal --buyers P/rounds/pitch-<n>/buyers.jsonl --options <ids> --batch-size <n> --out P/rounds/pitch-<n>/cards.jsonl` and copy its `seed` into `round.json`. Done when `round.json` validates and `cards.jsonl` has one line per held-out buyer.
6. **Interviews.** As RUN.md step 9, on the held-out buyers only, with the offer's card carrying the pitch in place of the one-sentence description: with more than three of them, the `orchestrate` skill when installed (job `buyer-panel-<slug>-pitch-<n>`, profile `light`, state dir `P/rounds/pitch-<n>/`, units = the batches in `cards.jsonl`, unit brief = INTERVIEW.md's worker brief for that batch's buyers, each worker writing only `P/rounds/pitch-<n>/answers/batch-<k>.jsonl`); otherwise, or without orchestrate, one buyer at a time with `interviews: inline`. Every context is fresh: no worker sees the source round, the quotes or another batch. Done when every held-out buyer has a valid answer.
7. **Judge.** As RUN.md step 10, in the round folder: `panel.py reactions P/rounds/pitch-<n> --buyers P/rounds/pitch-<n>/buyers.jsonl --out P/rounds/pitch-<n>/judge-input.jsonl`, a judge in its own context, `scores.jsonl`. Done when it validates.
8. **Numbers.** `panel.py aggregate P/rounds/pitch-<n> --buyers P/rounds/pitch-<n>/buyers.jsonl --out P/rounds/pitch-<n>/summary.json`, and again without `--json` for the table.
9. **Report.** Write `P/rounds/pitch-<n>/report.md`: the simulated-panel line (RUN.md's rules); the type and why (most likely, or named); the pitch with its trace table; the held-out buyers' first round (`quotes.json` `first_round`: BUY count, raw and discounted share) next to the try-out (`summary.json` `buy_share`), both copied, never computed; one row per held-out buyer, `| buyer | first round: decision, intent, reason | with the pitch: decision, intent, reason |`, from `quotes.json` `holdout` and `summary.json` `rows`; what the try-out buyers said against it (their pass reasons with counts). Say how many buyers the try-out had: with a handful, one flip is an anecdote, not a better pitch. End with a verdict (use the pitch on real people of this type, rework it, or try another type) and the first real test this week: say it to five people of this type and write down what they ask. With ai-slop-prose installed, sweep the report with purpose `panel report`, prose only, never a number, quote or table row.
10. **History.** Append one line to `P/history.md`: date, `pitch-<n>`, the type, the source round, BUY among the held-out buyers before and with the pitch, the verdict.

**Completion.** `quotes.json`, `pitch.md`, the try-out round's records and `summary.json` are in `P/rounds/pitch-<n>/`; the pitch is 65 to 85 words and every quoted phrase is traced to a buyer whose words hold it; no held-out buyer's words were borrowed; the pitch was swept or says it was not; every held-out buyer was interviewed in a fresh context; the report opens with the simulated-panel line, sets the try-out against the same buyers' first round from the script's numbers, and ends with a verdict and one real test; the history has the round's line.
